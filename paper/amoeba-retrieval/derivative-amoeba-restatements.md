## (a) Definition of Ronkin function and spectral potential
A (2511.11349, Eq. 9): phi(E) = lim_{N->inf} (1/N) ln|det T_N[E - h]|, T_N the Toeplitz matrix.
A (Eq. 14-15): phi(E) = min_mu R_sigma(mu),
  R_sigma(mu) = int_0^{2pi} dk/(2pi) ln|det sigma_mu(e^{ik})|,  sigma_mu(e^{ik}) = sigma(e^{mu+ik}), sigma = E - h(beta).
A (Eq. 17): with det sigma(beta) = C_E beta^{-p} prod_{j=1}^{p+q}[beta - beta_j(E)],
  R_sigma(mu) = ln|C_E| - p mu + sum_{j=1}^{p+q} max(mu, mu_j),  mu_j = ln|beta_j|.
B (2502.17931, Eq. 12-13): Phi(E) = min_mu R_E(mu),
  R_E(mu) = oint_{ln|beta|=mu} dbeta/(2 pi i beta) ln|ChP(E, beta)|, ChP(E,beta) = det[E - h_nB(beta)].
B (Eq. 15): R_E(mu) = ln|C_E| - p mu + sum_{j=1}^{2p} max(mu_j, mu).
  => B states: "the Ronkin function is a convex and piece-wise linear function, and its derivative is
     quantized to integer values in one-dimensional systems."
  (Note: B uses p=q, so 2p roots; A's a_{-M}beta^{-M}+...a_N beta^N maps to A's p,q.)
C (2407.01296, Eq. 8, 10-11): DOS rho = (1/2pi) Delta phi; phi(E) = (1/N) sum_i log|E_i - E|.
  C (Eq. 12): lim_N phi(E) = int_0^{2pi} dk/(2pi) log|det[H(e^{ik+mu}) - E]|, "according to Szegoe limit theorem",
     with mu chosen so |beta_p(E)| < e^mu < |beta_{p+1}(E)|.
  C (Eq. 13): lim_N phi(E) = sum_{j=p+1}^{p+q} log|beta_j(E)| + log|t_q|.
  C (Eq. 14): lim_N phi(E) = min_mu int_0^{2pi} dk/(2pi) log|det[H(e^{ik+mu}) - E]|, "forall E".

## (b) Spectrum / DOS from the minimization
A (verbatim): "This discussion is equivalent to the optimization problem phi(E) = min_mu R_sigma(mu)".
A (verbatim): "Using this compact expression, one finds that R_sigma(mu) attains its minimum within
   the interval [mu_p, mu_{p+1}], which precisely corresponds to the GBZ condition ... For energies on the
   OBC spectrum, this interval collapses to a single value of mu, whose magnitude gives the inverse
   localization length of the corresponding eigenstate."
A (Eq. 18): min_mu R_sigma(mu) = ln|C_E| + sum_{j=p+1}^{p+q} mu_j.
B (verbatim): "In the Amoeba formulation, the absence of an intermediate region where the Ronkin function
   is constant indicates that E lies within the spectrum, and the mu*, which minimizes the function, yields
   the corresponding eigenstate's inverse localization length. This criterion, known as 'central hole
   closing,' is consistent with the GBZ condition in Eq. (6)."
C (verbatim): "Guiding principle: Among all possible spectral deformations, the one with the minimum
   spectral potential corresponds to the potential generated by the non-Bloch bands."
C (verbatim): "Szegoe's limit theorem fails when E is inside the non-Bloch spectra in Eq. (12), ... the local
   form in Eq. (13) works for any E in the complex plane."
Toeplitz argument: A Sec. II.1 defines T_N[h]_{x,y} = int dk/(2pi) h(e^{ik}) e^{-ik(x-y)}; A Eq. (12)
  D_N^{(mu)} T_N[sigma] [D_N^{(mu)}]^{-1} = T_N[sigma_mu] with [D_N^{(mu)}]_{x,y} = delta_{x,y} e^{mu x};
  determinant invariant under similarity => optimization.
B does not use the Toeplitz/Szegoe route in the obtained text: it presents the general Szegoe theorem as a
  "conjecture" from Wang et al. 2024 and B's own GBZ-criterion derivation is deferred to its App. B (truncated).

## (d) Ronkin-minimum <-> winding-number-zero equivalence
No derivative source obtained states this as a theorem with a verbatim "equivalence" sentence; closest:

A (verbatim, following Eq. 17): "In this region, the transformed symbol sigma_mu is topologically trivial,

## (e) Stated limitations
B (verbatim): "Since the Ronkin function becomes even [Wang 2024], mu* = 0 always minimizes the Ronkin
   function. This seemingly implies that all bulk modes are delocalized, and the NHSE is absent in
   class-AII† systems. However, this conclusion contradicts both theoretical analyses and numerical results".
A (verbatim): "In multiband settings, the Ronkin function mixes contributions across bands; when degenerate
   states with different localization lengths occur, these contributions compete during the optimization of
   the Ronkin function, causing the standard Amoeba formulation to fail even in one dimension. This breakdown
   is unavoidable in the presence of symmetry-protected degeneracies, such as for TRS†, where Kramers pairs
   localize on opposite edges."
A (verbatim): "the mathematical properties of the Ronkin function are typically not preserved under bandwise
   separation."
A (verbatim): Kramers pairs "localize on opposite edges with inverse localization lengths +/- mu_1.
   Consequently, their contributions compete in the minimization in Eq. (14) and the conventional optimization
   formalism cannot be used."
Non-convexity of the optimization: NOT stated in any fetched text (B states convexity).
Spectral instability: NOT OBTAINED from A/B/C fetched text (only C's abstract mentions instability/non-convergence;
C verbatim abstract: "The instability drives the energy spectra towards the Amoeba spectra in the critical case.")
