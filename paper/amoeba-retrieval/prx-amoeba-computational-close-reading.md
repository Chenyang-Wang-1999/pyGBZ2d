## 1. What object is computed, and definitions
- **Amoeba [P]**, Eq. (14): `A_f = { log|β| : f(β) = 0 } ⊂ R^d`, with `log|β| ≡ (log|β_1|,…,log|β_d|)`; the Laurent polynomial used is `f = det[E − h(β)]`. Amoeba is closed; every hole is convex [P]. Name motivated by tentacles + vacuoles in 2D [P].
- **Ronkin function [P]**, Eq. (15): `R_f(μ) = ∫_{T^d} (dθ/2π)^d log| f(e^{μ+iθ}) |`, `T^d = [0,2π]^d`, `f(e^{μ+iθ}) ≡ f(e^{μ_1+iθ_1},…,e^{μ_d+iθ_d})`.
- **Gradient / winding numbers [P]**, Eqs. (16)–(18): `ν_j = ∂R_f/∂μ_j = Re ∫_{T^d}(dθ/2π)^d (∂_{μ_j} f)/f = Re ∫ (dθ/2π)^d (−i ∂_{θ_j} f)/f`; `w_j = (1/2πi)∮ dθ_j (∂_{θ_j}f)/f` is the winding number of the phase of f around the circle parametrised by θ_j (hence real); `ν_j = ∫_{T^{d−1}} (dθ_1…dθ_{j−1}dθ_{j+1}…dθ_d/(2π)^{d−1}) w_j` is the average of w_j over the (d−1)-torus. In 2D: `ν_1 = ∫_0^{2π}(dθ_2/2π) w_1`, `ν_2 = ∫(dθ_1/2π) w_2`. Each hole carries the integer tuple **"order of the hole"** `ν = (ν_1,…,ν_d)`; two holes cannot share an order; at most one hole has `ν = (0,…,0)`, the **"central hole"** [P].
- **Spectrum / DOS / GBZ**: the paper's goal is the OBC energy spectrum, DOS, "amoebic" universal spectrum and GBZ [P abstract/intro]. DOS and GBZ are obtained via the Ronkin function and Toeplitz matrix theory [P intro]. The **spectral potential** is never defined in the text I could read; derivative sources state the formulation as `Φ_Amoeba(E) = min_μ ∫ d^d k/(2π)^d log|f(e^{ik+μ},E)|` [D: Xiong–Xing–Hu arXiv:2407.01296, describing Ref. [65] = the PRX paper; the same expression appears in the Comms. Phys. SI snippet].
- **1D explicit (Jensen) formula [P]**, Eq. (19) and text: `(1/2π)∫_0^{2π}dθ log|g(Re^{iθ})| = log|g(0)| + Σ_{k=1}^{l} log|R/z_k|`, whose LHS "is exactly the Ronkin function R_g(log|R|)"; `ν = ∂R_g/∂(log|R|)` equals the number of zeros enclosed by `|β| = R`. The resulting explicit root formula appears as `R_g(log|R|) = log|g(0)| + log(|z_2|/|z_1|) + 2 log(|z_3|/|z_2|) + 3 log(|z_4|/|z_3|) + …` [P text + P-snip].

## 2. Input assumptions on the model [P]
1D: `H = Σ_{i,j=1}^{L} Σ_{a,b} |i,a⟩ (t_{j−i})_{ab} ⟨j,b|` (Eq. 1), translation-invariant bulk, **finite hopping range `n_c`** (`t_n = 0` for `|n| > n_c`), no Hermiticity required; `h(e^{ik}) = Σ_n t_n (e^{ik})^n`, continued as `h(β) = Σ_n t_n β^n`, `β = e^{μ+ik}`; `M` bands (band indices `a,b`). Paper claims universality "for models in arbitrary spatial dimensions, and with arbitrary degrees of freedom in a unit cell" [P]. Numerically demonstrated models: 1D non-Hermitian SSH (2 bands, quartic characteristic equation, `t_1=t_2=1, t_3=0.7, γ=4/3`) and a **single-band 2D** model with range-1 and diagonal hoppings (Eq. 12): `h(β)=t(β_x+β_x^{-1}+β_y+β_y^{-1}) + t'(β_x+β_x^{-1})(β_y+β_y^{-1}) + γ(β_x−β_x^{-1}+β_y−β_y^{-1})`, `t=1, t'=0.5, γ=0.2`. A non-Hermitian Chern-band model is used in Sec. VII (Fig. 2(b)) [P figure caption]. No "generic vs special case" restriction was stated in the parts I read; the "single band" simplification is explicit for the 2D demo ("For our single-band model, the determinant can be dropped").

## 3. Algorithm as the text allows (numbered); steps 1–4 are [P], steps 5–6 partly [P-snip]/[D]
1. Write `h(β)=Σ_n t_n β^n`; expand `det[E−h(β)] = a_{−M}(E)β^{−M} + … + a_N(E)β^N` with nonzero end coefficients; root moduli ordered `|β_1(E)| ≤ … ≤ |β_{M+N}(E)|` [P].
2. Form the amoeba `A_f = {log|β| : det[E−h(β)] = 0}` for the E of interest (in 2D this zero locus is real-2-dimensional and can be parametrised locally by β_x or β_y) [P].
3. Compute the Ronkin function `R_f(μ)` (Eq. 15) — equivalently via the gradient identity (16)–(18); in 1D use the closed-form root/Jensen expression (19). Every hole has constant integer order ν, and the Ronkin function is exactly linear on each hole; R_f is convex everywhere and finite everywhere [P].
4. Decide spectral membership by hole closing: **"the absence (presence) of a hole in the amoeba of the characteristic polynomial could be an indicator of the energy E being (not being) in the OBC energy spectrum"** [P]. Convexity gives: if the central hole exists the minimum lies on it, otherwise the minimum is at a single point inside the amoeba [P, Fig. 3 caption].
5. Toeplitz/Szegő machinery: the paper "make[s] use of this formulation and the theory of Toeplitz matrices to establish a universal way to determine the DOS as well as the GBZ" [P intro]; "The language of Toeplitz matrices is very useful in addressing tight-binding Hamiltonians" [P-snip]; the algebra estimate `‖𝒯[σ_1σ_2] − 𝒯[σ_1]𝒯[σ_2]‖_1 = O(L^{d−1})` appears [P-snip]; Appendix A is "A brief proof of Szegő's limit theorem" [P TOC].
6. Apply "the explicit formula of the Ronkin function to det[E − h(β)] = a_{−M}(E)β^{−M} + …" [P-snip] and minimise over μ to get the spectrum/DOS and the GBZ. Derivative restatements of that minimisation: `φ(E) = min_μ R_σ(μ)`, with `R_σ(μ)=∫_0^{2π}(dk/2π) ln|det σ_μ(e^{ik})|` and the minimum attained in `[μ_p, μ_{p+1}]` — "which precisely corresponds to the GBZ condition"; for E on the OBC spectrum the interval collapses to a single μ, whose value gives the inverse localisation length; the transformed symbol is "topologically trivial, corresponding to a vanishing winding number W[σ_μ]=0" [D: Kaneshiro–Peters, arXiv:2511.11349]; "central hole closing" as the spectrum criterion [D: arXiv:2502.17931]. **The PRX paper's own 2D GBZ definition/algorithm, any "self-conjugate"/corner/asymptotic-line argument, and any explicit optimisation/gradient-descent recipe were NOT OBTAINED.** A derivative source characterises the PRX GBZ as "a dD object embedded in a 2dD space … without specifying the geometric information" [D: 2407.01296].

## 7. Failure modes explicitly stated (readable ones)
[P] the 1D GBZ derivation "relying on the small rank of the coefficient matrix will become quite intractable" in higher dimensions; for 2D OBC "the number of constraint equations is proportional to the linear size L. It is therefore challenging to exploit all these boundary-condition equations", so "it is difficult to obtain a 2D counterpart of Eq. (9) from the approach similar to 1D"; [P] earlier 2D GBZ work was only approximate and "a general approach without resorting to uncontrolled approximations has been lacking"; [P, v1] "the recent finding of geometry-dependent NHSE in 2D non-Hermitian systems suggests that the GBZ might even not be definable in 2D". **The authors' own statements about non-convexity, optimisation difficulty, degeneracies, spectral instability, or cases where their method fails were NOT OBTAINED.** Derivative reports (not the PRX text): the amoeba/Ronkin optimisation "mixes contributions across bands" and "fails even in one dimension" for symmetry-protected degenerate pairs (Kramers pairs) [D: arXiv:2511.11349]; the amoeba formulation "neglects geometric information and yields geometry-irrelevant non-Bloch spectra" and spectra may be non-convergent/unstable in the presence of scale-free modes [D: arXiv:2407.01296].

## 9. "Ronkin minimum" vs "winding-number zero"
The equivalence is **not stated verbatim in any text I could read**. The primary basis is in Sec. III [P]: the Ronkin gradient `ν_j` **is** the average winding number `w_j` of the phase of `f` along θ_j (Eqs. 17–18); `ν` is constant on each hole; the unique hole with `ν = (0,…,0)` is the central hole, and [P, Fig. 3 caption] "when the central hole exists, the minimum is reached on the central hole; otherwise, the minimum is reached at a single point in the amoeba". Derivative statements of the same relation: "its derivative with respect to μ directly yields the winding number, W[σ_μ]" and "the transformed symbol σ_μ is topologically trivial, corresponding to a vanishing winding number W[σ_μ]=0" [D: arXiv:2511.11349].
